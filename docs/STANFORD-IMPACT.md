# Stanford evidence plan

Stanford describes admission as holistic and emphasizes depth, sustained commitment, intellectual vitality, and impact in context. The strongest story is not “I built an app” or an inflated download count. It is a documented cycle of hypothesis, field evidence, responsible engineering, measurable intervention, and honest iteration.

Official context: [Stanford undergraduate admission overview](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/overview/index.html).

## Evidence dossier

- Dated problem hypothesis and interview protocol
- De-identified interview table showing how the original consumer thesis changed
- Pilot protocol with cohort, dates, metrics, and kill thresholds fixed before launch
- Funnel: invited → joined → complete-room activated → week-two active → settlement confirmed
- Baseline/endline results with sample size and attrition
- Median reimbursement age and room-level activation, not page views
- Signed partner letter confirming cohort size, your role, dates, and observed result
- Two consented resident stories describing specific behavior change
- Git history, tests, architecture/security decisions, and technical retrospective
- Public three-to-five page pilot report including failures and limitations

## Claims rule

Every public/application claim needs a source artifact. Write “37 of 54 invited residents joined within 48 hours” only when the event export and cohort list support it. Never convert invitations into users, users into retained users, or self-reported improvement into proven financial savings.

## Strong narrative

“I began with a broad Splitwise-for-India idea. Interviews and competitor evidence showed that UPI was not a moat and group installation was the real barrier. I narrowed the product to a residence-sponsored first-month program, designed privacy boundaries, fixed settlement trust, preregistered pilot metrics, and changed the product based on the results.”
